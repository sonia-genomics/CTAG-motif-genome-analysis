# CHAPTER 3 — RESEARCH GAP AND OBJECTIVES

Although CTAG suppression has been widely reported,
its exact biological significance remains unresolved.

Most previous studies focused primarily on:

- Restriction systems
- DNA repair systems
- Mutation bias

However, limited work has investigated:

- Role of TAG stop codon suppression
- Coding-region specific CTAG depletion
- Local CTAG hotspot formation
- Base heterogeneity association
- Comparative genome-wide clustering
- Functional enrichment of hotspot regions

---

# Research Gap

There is insufficient understanding regarding:

- Why CTAG is more suppressed than other tetranucleotides
- Why TAG contributes disproportionately to suppression
- Why CTAG hotspots exist despite genome-wide depletion
- Whether CTAG clustering has biological significance

---

# Objectives

## Primary Objectives

- Analyze CTAG distribution across bacterial genomes
- Quantify CTAG under-representation using O/E statistics
- Compare CTAG against all tetranucleotide permutations

---

## Secondary Objectives

- Study TAG stop codon contribution
- Analyze coding-region specific suppression
- Investigate termination-site motif composition
- Detect local CTAG clustering
- Perform entropy and heterogeneity analysis
- Generate genome browser tracks
- Perform functional enrichment analysis
- Build automated Snakemake workflow

---

# Long-Term Goal

To better understand the evolutionary and biological significance
of CTAG suppression in bacterial genomes.
