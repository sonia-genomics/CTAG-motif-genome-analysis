# CHAPTER 6 — DISCUSSION

The results strongly support the hypothesis that CTAG suppression
is biologically driven rather than random.

The strong depletion of TAG-containing motifs suggests that
termination codon selection contributes significantly to CTAG rarity.

However, TAG suppression alone does not completely explain
the extreme depletion of CTAG.

The additional suppression caused by a preceding cytosine indicates
combined selective pressure on:

- Sequence structure
- Mutation repair
- Codon usage
- Palindromic organization

The presence of local CTAG clusters despite genome-wide depletion
suggests that CTAG motifs may still retain specialized biological roles.

These regions may be associated with:

- Regulatory hotspots
- Replication-associated structures
- Genome architecture
- Horizontally acquired regions

Entropy and heterogeneity analyses further support the idea that
CTAG clustering is linked with genomic organization.

Overall, CTAG suppression appears to result from multiple
evolutionary and structural constraints acting simultaneously.
