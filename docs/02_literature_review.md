# CHAPTER 2 — REVIEW OF LITERATURE

Genomic sequences are highly non-random and exhibit compositional
heterogeneity at multiple scales.

Short nucleotide motifs may become:

- Over-represented
- Under-represented

due to evolutionary, structural, and functional pressures.

---

# CTAG Under-Representation

CTAG is one of the most strongly suppressed tetranucleotide motifs
in bacterial genomes.

Previous studies suggest several contributing factors:

## 1. Restriction-Modification Systems

Restriction enzymes targeting CTAG-containing sequences may promote
negative selection against CTAG motifs.

Examples include:

- XbaI
- SpeI
- BlnI

---

## 2. VSP Repair System

The Very Short Patch repair system repairs T:G mismatches and may
reduce CTAG abundance during DNA repair processes.

---

## 3. DNA Methylation

DNA methylation can influence mutation rates and sequence composition.

Methylation-associated mutational bias may contribute to CTAG suppression.

---

## 4. Termination Codon Bias

CTAG contains the TAG stop codon.

TAG is generally the least preferred bacterial termination codon.

This suggests that stop codon selection may influence CTAG frequency.

---

## 5. Structural Constraints

Palindromic motifs may form secondary structures or DNA kinks that
can negatively affect genome stability.

---

# Local CTAG Clustering

Although globally suppressed, CTAG motifs sometimes occur in
localized high-density clusters.

Such clusters may have biological significance related to:

- Genome organization
- Replication
- Regulation
- Horizontal gene transfer

---

# Computational Genomics Approaches

Modern bioinformatics approaches enable:

- Genome-wide motif analysis
- Statistical enrichment
- Comparative genomics
- Local clustering analysis
- Functional annotation
- Multi-genome comparative analysis
