# CHAPTER 1 — INTRODUCTION

The diversity of life at molecular and cellular levels is fundamentally driven by
genetic information encoded in DNA sequences. Genomic DNA exhibits highly
non-random sequence organization, resulting in compositional heterogeneity across
living organisms.

One of the major manifestations of genomic heterogeneity is the over-representation
or under-representation of short nucleotide motifs.

A remarkable example of such sequence bias is the suppression of the CTAG
tetranucleotide motif in bacterial genomes.

CTAG is a palindromic tetranucleotide sequence that is significantly under-represented
in most bacterial genomes relative to expected frequencies.

The biological basis of CTAG suppression remains incompletely understood.

Several hypotheses have been proposed:

- Restriction-modification systems
- DNA methylation
- Very Short Patch (VSP) repair
- Structural instability
- Context-dependent mutations
- Stop codon-associated selection

This project investigates CTAG suppression using a large-scale computational
genomics framework integrating:

- Whole genome analysis
- Coding sequence analysis
- Termination site analysis
- Statistical analysis
- Comparative genomics
- Local clustering analysis
- Entropy analysis
- Functional enrichment
- Genome browser visualization

The project further investigates whether the TAG stop codon embedded inside
CTAG contributes significantly to its suppression.

Additionally, this work explores local CTAG hotspot regions and their possible
association with genomic heterogeneity and biological function.
